Sawyer Pheasant Tail Nymph

Nymph · beginner · Hooks #12–18 long shank nymph · Freshwater · Pattern by Frank Sawyer (Malcolm Price, Fly Dressers' Guild Pattern Library)

Video thumbnail from YouTube.

Rainbow TroutBrown Trout

Frank Sawyer's original, tied with fine copper wire instead of thread: a pheasant tail tail, body and double-layered wing case over a copper thorax hump. In different sizes it passes for a dozen kinds of nymph.

▶ Watch it tied on YouTube

Hatch & entomology

Imitates: Olive and other swimming mayfly nymphs (Baetidae)

When: All season on rivers; most useful from April to October

How to fish it

Cast upstream to sighted fish on clear rivers and let it sink, then lift it gently in front of the trout (the 'induced take').

Materials

  • Hook: Long-shank nymph hook, #12–18
  • Thread: Fine red copper wire (no thread)
  • Tail, body and wing case: Four long red-brown cock pheasant tail fibres

Step by step

  1. Cover the shank evenly with copper wire from bend to eye and lock it so it can't spin.
  2. Wind the wire back to just ahead of mid-shank and build a hump for the thorax, then take it to the bend.
  3. Tie in four pheasant tail fibres by the tips so about ⅛ inch sticks out as a tail.
  4. Twist the fibres with the wire for strength and wind them together in touching turns to the eye.
  5. Separate the fibres from the wire and take the wire back in open turns to just behind the thorax.
  6. Fold the fibres back over the thorax and secure them, then bring the wire forward and fold them forward again for a second layer.
  7. Tie off at the eye with half a dozen turns of wire, trim and break off the wire. The thorax should bulge and the body taper to the tail.
