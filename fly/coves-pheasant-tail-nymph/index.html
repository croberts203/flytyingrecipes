Cove's Pheasant Tail Nymph

Nymph (buzzer) · beginner · Hooks #10–14 heavy grub · Freshwater · Pattern by Arthur Cove (shown by Chris Reeves, Fly Dressers' Guild Pattern Library)

Rainbow TroutBrown Trout

Arthur Cove's early buzzer: a pheasant tail body with a silver wire rib, a pheasant tail wing case and a rabbit or hare thorax. It passes for a big midge, a sedge pupa or even small fry.

Hatch & entomology

Imitates: Chironomid pupa (buzzer) or general nymph

When: April–October on stillwaters

How to fish it

Fish it slowly on stillwaters during midge hatches. Try a seal's fur thorax or different body colours for variety.

Materials

  • Hook: Heavy grub hook, #10–14
  • Thread: Black 8/0
  • Body: Cock pheasant centre tail fibres (4+)
  • Rib: Fine silver wire
  • Wing case: Cock pheasant centre tail, 6–8 fibres
  • Thorax: Rabbit or hare's ear fur

Step by step

  1. Start the thread behind the eye and form a neat underbody.
  2. On the bend, tie in four pheasant tail fibres (more on bigger hooks; trim off the weak tips first) and the silver wire.
  3. Wrap the pheasant tail forward in touching turns to the thorax and remove the waste.
  4. Rib forward in even turns the opposite way to strengthen the body, then break off the excess wire.
  5. Tie in six to eight pheasant tail fibres by the butts on top of the thorax area.
  6. Dub a tight thorax of hare or rabbit (you can tease it out later for legs).
  7. Pull the wing case forward, tie it down at the eye and trim. Whip finish and varnish.
