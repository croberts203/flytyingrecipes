Clouser Minnow

Baitfish · beginner · Hooks #6 – 2/0 saltwater; #8 – #4 freshwater · Salt and fresh water

Photo: Mike Cline, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons. Example of the pattern, not tied by Fly Bench.

RedfishSpotted SeatroutFlounderSnookJack CrevalleLadyfishSpanish MackerelBluefishLargemouth BassBlack Crappie

Weighted, hook-point-up baitfish that jigs on the strip. Possibly the most versatile fly ever made — if you only tie one pattern, tie this.

How to fish it

Chartreuse/white for stained water, tan/white over grass flats, all-white around docks at night. Strip in short, sharp pulls and let it drop.

Materials

  • Hook: Saltwater stainless hook (e.g. SC15 / 34007), #2–1/0
  • Thread: Flat-waxed nylon or 140-denier, white
  • Eyes: Lead or tungsten dumbbell eyes, small–medium
  • Belly: Bucktail, white
  • Flash: Krystal Flash or Flashabou, pearl or gold
  • Wing: Bucktail, chartreuse or tan
  • Head: UV resin or head cement

Step by step

  1. Start the thread about one-third of the shank back from the eye and build a small thread bump.
  2. Lash the dumbbell eyes on top of the shank just behind the bump with tight figure-eight wraps, then add a drop of glue.
  3. Turn the hook so the eyes are underneath. Tie in a sparse clump of white bucktail in front of the eyes, then cross behind them and tie it down along the shank. It should extend about 2–3 shank lengths past the bend.
  4. Flip the hook over (point up). In front of the eyes, tie in 8–12 strands of flash, about as long as the bucktail.
  5. Tie in a sparse clump of colored bucktail on top of the flash. Keep the whole fly sparse — less hair sinks faster and looks more alive.
  6. Build a neat tapered head, whip finish, and coat the head and thread wraps with UV resin or cement.
