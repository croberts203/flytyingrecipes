Ace of Spades

Lure (Matuka) · advanced · Hooks #6–10 long shank · Freshwater · Pattern by David Collyer (dressing shown by Paul Little, Flydresser Spring 2009)

Video thumbnail from YouTube.

Rainbow TroutBrown Trout

A 1960s stillwater lure from Surrey with a black body, black hen hackles bound down Matuka-style, a guinea fowl throat and a bronze mallard roof over the top.

▶ Watch it tied on YouTube

Hatch & entomology

Imitates: Leech, fry or large dark nymph; a general stillwater lure

When: All season; best early spring and autumn on reservoirs

How to fish it

Best on an intermediate line with long, slow draws or a slow figure-of-eight. If nothing follows, speed up. On a floater, add a little flat lead under the body and lengthen the leader.

Materials

  • Hook: Long shank lure hook (e.g. Partridge Bomber), #8
  • Thread: Black
  • Rib: Oval silver tinsel
  • Body: Black Vernille or rayon chenille
  • Wing: 4 black hen hackles, matched pairs
  • Throat: Guinea fowl, large-spotted feather
  • Roof: Bronze mallard slips (left and right)
  • Head: Head varnish

Step by step

  1. Start the thread a short way behind the eye and tie in the oval silver tinsel along the shank, taking the thread back to just short of the hook point.
  2. Strip the fluff from a length of Vernille, tie it in at the rear and wind it forward as a smooth body, leaving room for the head. Strip the core at the tie-in point to keep the head slim.
  3. Pair four hen hackles, two from each side of the cape so their curves match. Strip the underside fibres along the body length so they sit flat on the back.
  4. Lay the hackles along the top of the body and bind them down Matuka-style with open turns of the rib, using a dubbing needle to free trapped fibres.
  5. Now add a throat of guinea fowl about half the body length. (Adding the throat before the wing gets in the way of the rib, so do it here.)
  6. Take a slip from a left-hand bronze mallard feather and set it on the near side with a loose pinch wrap, pulling the thread up slowly so the fibres don't crease. Repeat on the far side with a right-hand slip.
  7. Trim the butts, build a neat head with flattened thread, whip finish and give it two coats of varnish.
