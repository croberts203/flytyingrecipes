Simple Shrimp

Shrimp · beginner · Hooks #4 – #1 · Saltwater

Photo: Mike Cline, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons. Example of the pattern, not tied by Fly Bench.

RedfishSpotted SeatroutFlounderSheepsheadSnook

A lightly weighted shrimp with a hackled body and long antennae. Shrimp are the most common prey on NE Florida flats and creeks.

How to fish it

Fish slowly on the bottom with short hops. Tan, pink and olive work. Great under dock lights for seatrout and around grass edges for reds.

Materials

  • Hook: Saltwater hook, #2–#1
  • Thread: 140-denier, tan
  • Eyes: Small bead-chain or mono shrimp eyes
  • Antennae: Krystal Flash and a couple of long rubber legs
  • Mouth parts: Craft fur or bucktail, tan
  • Body: Dubbing, tan, with a palmered grizzly hackle
  • Shell: Thin clear strip or a coat of UV resin over the back

Step by step

  1. Lay a thread base and tie in bead-chain eyes on top of the shank near the bend.
  2. Tie in a sparse clump of craft fur extending past the bend, with flash and rubber legs for antennae.
  3. Tie in a grizzly hackle by the tip at the bend.
  4. Dub a slightly tapered body forward to just behind the hook eye.
  5. Palmer the hackle forward through the dubbing, tie off and trim the top fibers flat.
  6. Whip finish and coat the back with a thin layer of UV resin for a shell.
