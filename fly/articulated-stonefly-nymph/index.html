Articulated Stonefly Nymph

Nymph (realistic) · advanced · Hooks #10 straight (abdomen) + #10 curved (thorax) · Freshwater · Pattern by Sergio Córdoba, Argentina (Fly Dressers' Guild Pattern Library)

Illustration by Fly Bench showing the general look of the pattern.

Rainbow TroutBrown Trout

A semi-realistic, two-hook articulated stonefly nymph with a segmented vinyl abdomen, scud-back wing pads, silicone legs and epoxy eyes. The rear hook is cut off, leaving a hinged body that wiggles.

Hatch & entomology

Imitates: Large stonefly nymph (Perlidae / Perlodidae)

When: Nymphs all year in rivers; most active before the spring and early-summer hatches

How to fish it

Bounce it along the bottom of fast, rocky runs where big stoneflies live. Add weight in the eyes or body for deep water.

Materials

  • Hooks: #10 straight hook for the abdomen, #10 curved hook for the thorax
  • Thread: White 8/0
  • Tails and antennae: Monofilament, tapered with a scalpel
  • Abdomen: Yellow rabbit dubbing with a clear vinyl rib
  • Wing pads and thorax cover: Scud Back or other clear strip
  • Thorax: Brown craft fur and yellow dubbing
  • Legs: Silicone legs, 3 pairs
  • Eyes: Epoxy
  • Shading: Permanent markers
  • Joint: Monofilament

Step by step

  1. Taper short lengths of mono with a scalpel for the tails and antennae.
  2. On the abdomen hook, tie in the two tails and colour them with a marker.
  3. Tie in the vinyl rib, dub yellow rabbit to the eye, then wrap the vinyl forward in touching turns to make a segmented abdomen. Tie off and cut the hook off behind the body.
  4. On the thorax hook, lay mono from the eye to the bend, thread it through the abdomen hook's eye and bring it back along the shank. Fold both ends back and bind them down so the joint is secure.
  5. Tie a tuft of craft fur at the bend of the thorax hook, then tie in the three pairs of silicone legs and dub around the hind legs.
  6. Cut the hind and fore wing pads from Scud Back. Tie the hind pad on top between the back two pairs of legs and colour it.
  7. Dub around the middle legs, add a tuft of craft fur on top, then tie the fore wing pad between the middle and front legs with another small tuft of fur.
  8. Tie in a strip of Scud Back at the base of the fur, tie the antennae in an X at the eye and dub behind them to build the head.
  9. Pull the Scud Back forward over the front of the thorax and then the head, tying it down at each.
  10. Shade the wing case, thorax, head, antennae and legs with markers. Kink the legs with a warm needle and build the eyes with epoxy.
