The Carson

Lough wet fly · intermediate · Hooks #10 wet · Freshwater · Pattern by Irish lough pattern via Norman Wells (Chris Reeves, Flydresser Summer 2024)

Rainbow TroutBrown Trout

A heavily dressed Irish lough fly: orange and bronze mallard tail, golden olive seal's fur body with a palmered golden olive hackle, an orange hen shoulder, a bronze mallard wing and a front hackle of golden olive mallard flank.

Hatch & entomology

Imitates: Hatching sedge or general lough insect life; an attractor

When: Summer on the loughs, especially in a good wave; mayfly and sedge time

How to fish it

Short-lined as the top dropper in a team of three from a drifting boat in a big wave. It's built to stick in the film when you dibble it.

Materials

  • Hook: Strong wet hook (e.g. Partridge G3AY), #10
  • Thread: Fluorescent green 70 denier
  • Tail: Orange cock fibres under bronze mallard
  • Rib: Fine oval gold tinsel or gold wire
  • Body: Golden olive seal's fur
  • Body hackle: Golden olive cock saddle
  • Shoulder: Orange hen
  • Wing: Bronze mallard fibres
  • Front hackle: Grey mallard flank dyed pale golden olive

Step by step

  1. Lay a thread base. Tie in a bunch of orange cock fibres about as long as the shank, curving up, and bind the butts along the shank.
  2. Tie a bunch of bronze mallard of equal length on top, then the rib, covering the waste for an even underbody.
  3. Dub a tight golden olive seal's fur body.
  4. Tie in the golden olive hackle at the head, wind it back in open turns, then rib forward through it. Trim.
  5. Wind two turns of orange hen at the shoulder.
  6. Tie in a small bunch of bronze mallard on top as the wing.
  7. Wind two or three turns of golden olive mallard flank, stroking the fibres back. Form a neat head, whip finish and varnish.
