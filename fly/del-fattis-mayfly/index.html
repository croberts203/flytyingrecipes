Del Fatti's Mayfly

Mayfly (dun) · advanced · Hooks #10–12 curved grub · Freshwater · Pattern by Pat Del Fatti (Flydresser Spring 2021)

Illustration by Fly Bench showing the general look of the pattern.

Rainbow TroutBrown Trout

A natural-looking mayfly dun with a detached foam body made on a needle, moose tails, dun and olive hackles clipped flat underneath and wings of burned mallard or yellow-dyed elk. It lands right side up and floats well.

Hatch & entomology

Imitates: Mayfly (Ephemera danica), sub-imago dun

When: Late May to mid-June ('duffer's fortnight'), mostly afternoons and evenings

How to fish it

For a true mayfly hatch on rivers and lakes. The elk hairwing version is the one Pat says really works, and it won't twist your leader like burned wings can in big sizes.

Materials

  • Hook: Curved grub hook (e.g. Partridge K4AY), #10–12
  • Thread: Tan or light cahill 8/0
  • Extended body: Razor foam 0.5mm, coloured with markers
  • Tail: Three moose hairs
  • Glue: Flexible waterproof (silicone) glue
  • Thorax: Pale yellow-olive dubbing
  • Hackles: Chinese cock, grey dun and light olive
  • Wings: Mallard flank (burned to shape) or yellow-dyed elk hair

Step by step

  1. Cut a foam strip 5–5.5mm wide and cut half its width away along 30–35mm.
  2. Put a long needle in the vise with 30–35mm sticking out and start the thread on it.
  3. Tie the narrow part of the foam to the needle in open spirals, not too tight, then tie in three moose hairs for tails. Spiral the thread down and add glue.
  4. Wind the wide part of the foam back over it, each wrap half covering the last (about ten wraps). Tie it down and tie off.
  5. Gently push the finished body off the needle without crushing it, let the glue dry and colour it with markers.
  6. Put a hook in the vise, cover the shank with thread and dub a small ball at the rear. Tie the body on with tight wraps, trim and cover the join with dubbing.
  7. Tie in both hackles by their tips, crease the fibres back by running a scissor edge along the stems, wind them and cover with a little dubbing to make a shoulder.
  8. Burn two mallard feathers to wing shape (or tie in a bunch of yellow elk). Tie them in with the good sides facing each other so they flare apart.
  9. Bind the wing stalks tight, dub over them, clip the hackle flat underneath and whip finish.
