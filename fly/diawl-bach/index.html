Diawl Bach

Nymph (buzzer) · beginner · Hooks #10–16 · Freshwater · Pattern by Traditional Welsh pattern (Malcolm Price, Fly Dressers' Guild Pattern Library)

Rainbow TroutBrown Trout

The 'little devil': a single peacock herl body with a copper rib, a red game tail and throat. Simple, slim and one of the most reliable stillwater flies there is.

Hatch & entomology

Imitates: Chironomid midge pupa (buzzer), ascending to the surface

When: All season on stillwaters, peaking April–June and again in September; best in calm mornings and evenings

How to fish it

Fish it slowly on a floating or midge-tip line, on its own or on a washing-line rig, whenever buzzers are about on reservoirs.

Materials

  • Hook: Standard wet fly or nymph hook, #10–16
  • Thread: Black
  • Tail: 10–12 red game cock fibres, as long as the body
  • Rib: Medium copper wire
  • Body: One strand of peacock herl
  • Throat: 10–12 red game cock fibres, reaching the hook point

Step by step

  1. Start the thread near the eye and run it back in touching turns to just short of the bend.
  2. Tie in 10–12 red game fibres for a tail as long as the body, binding the butts along the shank for a smooth underbody.
  3. Tie in the copper wire just behind the eye and bind it down along the shank to the tail.
  4. Tie in one peacock herl by the tip (cut off the weak last inch) and wind it forward in touching turns to the eye.
  5. Rib forward in open turns the opposite way to the herl, tie off and break off the waste.
  6. Tie in a throat of 10–12 red game fibres that reach the hook point. Neaten the head, whip finish and varnish.
