Robjent's Daddy

Daddy-long-legs (dry) · intermediate · Hooks #10 nymph · Freshwater · Pattern by Alistair Robjent, Stockbridge (shown by Ian McKenzie, Flydresser Spring 2021)

Rainbow TroutBrown Trout

The chalkstream daddy sold by the jarful in Stockbridge: a foam underbody wrapped with peacock herl, knotted pheasant tail legs, hackle-tip wings and a dense red-brown cock hackle. A proven One Fly Competition fly on the Test.

Hatch & entomology

Imitates: Crane fly (daddy-long-legs, Tipulidae), adult on the surface

When: Late July to October, peaking in September on breezy days

How to fish it

Fish it dry on rivers and lakes from late summer, when crane flies are blown onto the water.

Materials

  • Hook: Nymph hook (e.g. Kamasan B830), #10
  • Thread: Black 8/0
  • Underbody: Thin closed-cell foam strip, 3–4mm
  • Body: Four peacock herls
  • Legs: Knotted pheasant tail, four each side
  • Wings: Two cock hackle tips
  • Hackle: Two red-brown Indian cock hackles

Step by step

  1. Take the thread to above the barb and tie in a 3–4mm strip of foam (any colour; it won't show).
  2. Wrap the foam forward in slightly overlapping turns to make a tapered body.
  3. Return the thread to the rear, tie in four peacock herls, twist them around the thread for strength and wind them over the foam in touching turns.
  4. Tie in four knotted pheasant tail legs on each side.
  5. Lay two cock hackles flat along the body and tie them in (they turn 90° as you wind), then tie in two hackle points as wings.
  6. Wind several hackle turns behind the wings and a few in front. Whip finish and add a drop of varnish.
