Whiskey Fly

Lure · beginner · Hooks #6–12 3X long · Freshwater · Pattern by Albert Willock (shown by Ian McKenzie, Flydresser Winter 2015)

Illustration by Fly Bench showing the general look of the pattern.

Rainbow Trout

A bright 1970s stillwater lure: silver body ribbed with fluorescent red, an orange throat and a hot orange calf tail wing. Rainbows still can't leave it alone.

Hatch & entomology

Imitates: Attractor lure; suggests a small fry

When: Summer, especially when trout chase fry; works in bright conditions

How to fish it

Strip it on a floating or intermediate line for stocked rainbows, especially in coloured water. Many small stillwaters limit lures to a size 10 long shank.

Materials

  • Hook: 3X long shank (e.g. Kamasan B800), #6–12
  • Thread: Red 8/0
  • Butt and rib: Fluorescent red floss (Glo-Brite No. 4) or the red thread
  • Body: Flat silver tinsel
  • Throat: Hot orange cock hackle fibres
  • Wing: Hot orange calf tail
  • Coating: Hard varnish or UV resin over the body

Step by step

  1. Run the thread down to above the barb and tie in the fluorescent red floss.
  2. Tie in the flat silver tinsel, take the thread back to the eye and wind a smooth tinsel body, leaving room behind the eye for the throat and wing.
  3. Make a small butt with the floss, then rib it up the body and tie off. Coat the body with varnish or thin resin and let it set.
  4. Tie in a throat of orange hackle fibres that just reaches the hook point.
  5. Add a wing of hot orange calf tail, build a prominent red head, whip finish and varnish.
