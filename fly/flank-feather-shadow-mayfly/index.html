Flank Feather Shadow Mayfly

Mayfly (emerger) · intermediate · Hooks #12–16 light grub · Freshwater · Pattern by Geoff Wilcox (Fly Dressers' Guild Pattern Library)

Video thumbnail from YouTube.

Rainbow TroutBrown Trout

A one-feather emerger: a teal or mallard flank feather forms the tail, body and wing, with a few turns of peacock herl at the thorax. The grub hook hangs through the film while the body sits on it.

▶ Watch it tied on YouTube

Hatch & entomology

Imitates: Mayfly or large olive (Ephemeroptera) emerging through the surface film

When: Late spring through summer; olives April–October, true mayfly late May–June

How to fish it

Fish it as an emerging dun when fish are taking just under the surface. Trim the underside so it sits upright; too tall and it falls on its side.

Materials

  • Hook: Light-wire grub hook, #12–16
  • Thread: Tan 8/0 or 6/0
  • Body, tail and wing: One teal or mallard flank feather
  • Thorax: Peacock herl
  • Hackle (variant): Cock hackle about 2.5× the hook gape

Step by step

  1. Tie on a short bed of thread, about 4mm long.
  2. Strip the fluff from a flank feather. Holding it by the tip, stroke about three-quarters of the fibres back; the tip becomes the tail.
  3. Pinch the stroked feather to set the tie-in point and fix it to the hook with two pinch-and-loop wraps.
  4. Lift the tail and body and put two turns behind; lift the wing and put two turns in front, so the feather is split into body and wing.
  5. Stand the stem upright and lock it with a couple of wraps, smoothing the fibres back.
  6. Tie in a peacock herl, wind four turns as a thorax and tie off.
  7. Trim the wing, body and tail to shape. (Variant: use a smaller feather and wind a cock hackle through the split wing, two turns behind and two in front, then trim it flat underneath.)
