Green Machine

Salmon fly (bug) · advanced · Hooks #6 · Freshwater · Pattern by Warren Duncan, New Brunswick (Flydresser Winter 2009)

Atlantic Salmon

Warren Duncan's Miramichi bug: a silver tag, fluorescent green and red butt, Krystal Flash tail and a spun, clipped deer hair body with a palmered hackle drawn into it.

How to fish it

A famous Miramichi salmon fly that works wherever anglers are brave enough to try it.

Materials

  • Hook: Salmon hook (e.g. Partridge Bomber), #6
  • Thread: White to start, strong GSP or Kevlar for spinning, then head colour
  • Tag: Fine oval silver tinsel
  • Butt: Fluorescent green floss and fluorescent red wool
  • Tail: Small bunch of Krystal Flash
  • Hackle: Short-fibred saddle hackle, palmered
  • Body: Spun deer hair, clipped
  • Guard: 12mm of silicone tube (to protect the tail while trimming)

Step by step

  1. With white thread, wind a fine oval silver tag ending in line with the point. Lay a 4mm bed of thread in front of it.
  2. Tie in two strands of green floss trailing back over the tag and one strand at the front. Build a green butt with them, then tie in fluorescent red wool and wrap it over the front part so green and red end up equal. Trim but keep the thread.
  3. Tie in a small bunch of Krystal Flash in front of the butt, reaching the bend, and cut away the white thread.
  4. Start strong GSP or Kevlar thread. Strip 12mm of fibres from the butt of a short saddle hackle and anchor the bare stem firmly.
  5. Slide a 12mm piece of silicone tube over the tail, tag and hackle stem to protect them.
  6. Spin small bunches of combed deer hair one at a time, compressing each firmly back (six to eight bunches), stopping 1.5–2mm from the eye.
  7. Trim the hair roughly all round, then fine-tune the shape. Slide the silicone tube off.
  8. Change to your head thread colour and wind the hackle forward in open turns, pulling it down into the clipped body. Make one extra turn, tie off, trim and varnish.
