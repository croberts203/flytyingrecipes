Big Red

Dry fly (emerging midge) · beginner · Hooks #10 (#12–16 in other colours) · Freshwater · Pattern by Bill Latham and the Bath & District team (Ian McKenzie, Flydresser Winter 2020)

Video thumbnail from YouTube.

Rainbow TroutBrown Trout

A Shipman's Buzzer with a parachute: a finely dubbed red seal's fur body with wire rib, white poly breathers and a post pulled forward over a soft parachute hackle. It's a stuck, crippled midge.

▶ Watch it tied on YouTube

Hatch & entomology

Imitates: Chironomid midge emerging, crippled and stuck in the surface film

When: Spring to autumn on stillwaters; big red midges in early season, other colours through summer

How to fish it

Grease the hackle and breathers but let the tail dip below the surface. Use a visible dry on the dropper as a sighter and watch for swirls. A Bristol reservoirs, Rutland, Grafham and Draycote staple; tie other colours on #12–16.

Materials

  • Hook: Kamasan B170 or similar, #10 (#12–16 for other colours)
  • Thread: To match the body
  • Breathers and post: White polypropylene or Glo-Brite multi yarn
  • Rib: Fine silver or gold wire
  • Body: Finely dubbed red seal's fur
  • Hackle: Soft cock or natural furnace hen

Step by step

  1. Run the thread to above the barb. Tie in the poly yarn leaving a short tail, bind it toward the eye, stopping 2–3mm short, then stand it up and wrap around its base for a post.
  2. Tie in the hackle by the tip at the post (not too stiff a cock hackle) and run the thread back to the tail, tying in the wire as you go.
  3. Dub a fine seal's fur body forward to the post and rib it in open turns, tying off in front of the post.
  4. Wind five or six turns of hackle around the post and tie down.
  5. Take one turn of dubbing in front of the post, pull the post forward and tie it down at the eye. Whip finish and trim the breathers to length.
