6-inch UV Pike Fly

Pike streamer (synthetic) · advanced · Hooks 4/0–6/0 predator · Freshwater · Pattern by Alois Trübner (Flydresser Spring 2021)

Northern Pike

A big-profile but light all-synthetic pike streamer. A half rubber bead behind the eye props up a short layer of Curly Creature Hair so a little material makes lots of volume; a curly dubbing head pushes water, with glow eyes under UV resin. It doesn't soak up water and lasts nearly a season.

How to fish it

Fish it with discreet jerks and light strips just under the surface; it casts on a #6 rod. Natural colours most of the time, a bright one in winter. Put a little more material underneath for a keel, and brush it back into shape after each fish.

Materials

  • Hook: Predator hook (e.g. Partridge Bad Boy Predator 4/0 or wide-gape 6/0)
  • Bead: 6mm oval rubber bead, cut in half
  • Thread: Strong 100 denier predator thread
  • Body: Curly Creature Hair, black and pearl grey, with black holographic flash
  • Lateral line: Lateral Scale, pearl black
  • Head: Curly Creature Dubbing, black
  • Eyes: 8mm glow-in-the-dark eyes
  • Finish: Superglue (gel and liquid) and UV resin (medium, thin, flex fluo orange)
  • Tools: Hair straightener, hair clips, hollow tube, UV torch

Step by step

  1. Cut the rubber bead in half and thread it on flat side toward the eye. Lay 1.5cm of thread behind the eye with a small bump at the end, glue it and push the bead onto the bump.
  2. Take a pinch of body hair (about a toothpick's thickness for 6/0), cut it to 16–18cm and taper both ends by rolling and pulling.
  3. Straighten about 10cm of one end and 3–4cm of the other with the hair straightener, just until the fibres change, then switch it off.
  4. Tie the material in front of the bead, long end forward, two-thirds along its length. Spread it round the shank with a little more on top, then tighten.
  5. Brush the fibres, flip the long ends back with a hollow tube so they cloak the short ones, and tie them down hard against the bead.
  6. Add lateral scale on each side, clip everything back out of the way and wick liquid superglue into the wraps.
  7. Cut head dubbing to about 1.3× the body length and tie it on top with two-thirds facing back. Turn the hook over and add a slightly shorter bunch underneath the same way. Glue the wraps.
  8. Pluck the dubbing apart all round the shank and flip the forward fibres back with the tube. Make a few supporting wraps in front and whip finish.
  9. Glue on the eyes and cover them with UV resin for a durable head.
