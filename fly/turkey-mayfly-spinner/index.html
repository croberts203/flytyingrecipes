Turkey Mayfly Spinner

Mayfly (spent spinner) · intermediate · Hooks #10–12 long shank dry · Freshwater · Pattern by Chris Reeves (Fly Dressers' Guild Pattern Library)

Rainbow TroutBrown Trout

Chris Reeves' most effective spent mayfly: three moose tails, a black peacock butt, a flat white turkey herl body, white calf tail wings tied spent and a black hackle figure-eighted through the wing roots.

Hatch & entomology

Imitates: Mayfly spent spinner (Ephemera danica, the 'spent gnat')

When: Late May to mid-June, evening spinner falls

How to fish it

Fish it in the evening fall of spent mayfly, flat in the film where trout are sipping dying spinners.

Materials

  • Hook: Long-shank dry hook, #10–12
  • Thread: Black or white
  • Tail: Three moose body hairs (or peccary)
  • Butt: Two turns of black-dyed peacock herl
  • Body: Four white turkey herls, wound flat
  • Wings: White calf tail, split and tied spent
  • Hackle: Black cock, a size small, about eight turns

Step by step

  1. Stack a bunch of white calf tail and tie it in facing forward about 2mm back from the eye. Split it and figure-eight it into the spent position, each wing as long as the shank. Trim the waste at an angle and bind back to the tail.
  2. Tie in three moose hairs, divided, about 1.5 times the body length.
  3. Wind two turns of black peacock herl as a butt.
  4. Wind four white turkey herls flat in touching turns to the wings (change hands so they don't twist) and tie off.
  5. Tie in a long black hackle, a size small for the hook. Wind five or six turns behind the wings, figure-eight over each wing root, and finish with one turn at the eye.
  6. Whip finish and varnish the knot.
